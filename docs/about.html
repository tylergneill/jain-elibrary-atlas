About

The Jain eLibrary is an online library of Jain literature run by JAINA's education arm. jainqq.org ("Jain Quantum") is its full-text search engine, which also publishes the OCR text of each item it indexes. The library has OCR'd more of its books than Quantum shows, and this Atlas reads both.

This unofficial “atlas” interface — not affiliated in any way — offers a better way to explore the Sanskrit books in this large and mostly vernacular library. It is an "atlas" in the sense that it not only charts the navigable waters — with more transparent browsing by author and by canonical text — but also plumbs their depths — with visibility into sizes, counts, and changes over time that the source collection doesn't surface on its own. It is not a mirror, as it hosts no text of its own; instead, every entry links back to jainqq.org for the actual content.

For more perspective on this and sibling Atlas projects, see Sāgarasaṅgama.

Practical Intro to Jain eLibrary Structure

One Library, Two Overlapping Sites

The library has two public faces over one catalogue, in which every item has the same six-digit serial number on both.

jainelibrary.org is the library proper: the fuller catalogue record for each item, and its PDF files, which can be downloaded only with an account. jainqq.org is the search engine: a thinner record, a page-by-page viewer, and the whole OCR text of the item on a single page, with no account needed.

PDFs and Two Text Sources

Every item is a PDF of a printed book, and the PDF is the primary object. Text exists only as OCR of that PDF, and there are two sources of it:

  • Quantum text: the public text on jainqq.org, one page per book, no account needed. of the Sanskrit books have one.
  • eLibrary text: the library's own OCR, a Word file beside the PDF, downloadable only with an account. of the books have one.

The two are not the same text. Each eLibrary file opens with a note saying when it was made, and every one this Atlas fetched says the same day, : the library re-ran OCR over its whole backlog that day, books added years earlier included. Quantum's text comes from an earlier pass over the same scans. On five books that have both, the two are the same length to within a few percent, but only 63–97% of their words match in order. They divide words differently (श्री हेमचंद्राचार्य against श्रीहेमचंद्राचार्य) and misread different letters; neither is clearly the better.

They also stop at different times (see Where the Text Stops): Quantum's text ends with books that went online in , the library's OCR in . So books have eLibrary text but no Quantum text. The Atlas uses Quantum's text wherever there is one and the library's for those (fetched with an account; see Scraping), and says which on every book: Quantum texts and eLibrary texts. Because the two are separate OCR passes, their sizes are comparable but not identical, and a reader who opens a book's Quantum page sees Quantum's reading, not the library's. The other books have no OCR anywhere.

For example, पञ्चसंग्रह (serial 001014) is a 250-page book printed in 1927: its PDF is in the library, and its text is on Quantum.

Metadata

The catalogue is built around how a book is stored, not what it is about. classification is mostly script and format (Book_Devnagari, Manuscript, Dictionary), written as a comma-separated list; the Sanskrit books alone carry distinct strings. Subject terms exist on some records but neither site offers them for browsing.

script names a language rather than a script: of the Sanskrit books say Hindi, meaning Devanāgarī. language is a comma-separated string as catalogued (Sanskrit, or Sanskrit, Hindi, typically for a text with a Hindi translation or commentary).

A "Manuscripts" Folder of Printed Books

The library files of the Sanskrit books under "manuscripts." These are not handwritten: they are block prints and printed books in horizontal format. of them have a Quantum text.

Authors and Editors

The catalogue's author is whoever the record was filed under, which is often a modern editor or publisher rather than the author of the work. A separate field for the classical author exists but is filled on only of the Sanskrit books (%). Names are romanised without diacritics and not standardised, and a single field often holds several people.

Canonical Texts

The one subject-like structure the catalogue does carry is the Śvetāmbara आगम canon: a tag of the form agam_… names the canonical text an item belongs to. of the Sanskrit books (%) carry one.

Sanskrit vs. Other Languages

The library is mostly Gujarati and Hindi. Its two catalogues together list items — books, articles, magazines and audio — of which have a text. Among the books, mention Sanskrit among their languages, are in Sanskrit, Prakrit or both and nothing else, and are catalogued as Sanskrit alone.

Obtaining and Representing Jain eLibrary Data

Scraping

Metadata comes from both sites. Jain Quantum's catalog is 34 pages of a thousand rows each, behind a Cloudflare challenge which an ordinary browser session passes. The library's own records come from its public catalogue interface.

Text comes from Jain Quantum wherever it has one: one request for each Sanskrit book with a text. With polite scraping (2 seconds per request), this takes a little over an hour.

For the books Quantum has no text for but the library OCR'd, the library's own Word copy was fetched instead, through an account on jainelibrary.org, one file every 2 seconds and within the library's daily download limit. Each is kept as the library served it and as plain text derived from it.

Texts from either source are fetched for the purpose of calculating size and none is shared publicly. No PDFs were fetched at all.

Sanskrit Books Only

The Atlas covers the items catalogued as Sanskrit and nothing else which are books — including the "manuscripts" folder described above and Jain Quantum's separate shelf of आगम editions. Editions that pair a Sanskrit text with a translation are left out: Sanskrit and Hindi share a script here, so a size figure for such a book would not be a Sanskrit figure.

Even so, many books catalogued as Sanskrit alone carry a Hindi preface or apparatus, so the figures here are somewhat high for that reason too. The cataloguing convention has also shifted: recent Sanskrit editions are mostly entered as "Sanskrit, Hindi" and so fall outside this selection.

Calculating Size Information

Size calculation involves two steps:

  1. stripping page markers, bare page numbers, and the stamps of the digitising libraries which the OCR read off each page, and
  2. transliterating from Devanāgarī to IAST.

IAST text takes up less space (here % of the cleaned text's byte count) and is more common for cross-collection size comparisons.

Every one of the texts has been measured, from Quantum and from the library's OCR: pages,  MB as IAST, with a median book of about  KB. This is OCR output with its noise still in; only whole lines that are plainly not content are removed.

Calculating Date Information

Every record on jainelibrary.org carries the day the item went online, so dates here are measured rather than inferred. The books known only to Jain Quantum have no such record and carry no date.

This is the date an item joined the library, not the date of the book: the publication year, where the catalogue gives one, is shown separately.

Browsing by Author and Canon

The default view is by author. Where the catalogue names a classical author, the book is listed under that name, and the editor it was filed under is kept on an "ed." tag; otherwise it is listed under whatever name the catalogue gives. % of the books have a name of one kind or the other. No normalization is performed, so the catalogue's variant spellings of one person remain separate here.

Alternatively, the books can be browsed by canon: the six classes of the आगम in their traditional order (Aṅga, Upāṅga, Chedasūtra, Mūlasūtra, Cūlikāsūtra, Prakīrṇaka), then the canonical text. Placement follows the library's own agam_… tags and nothing else; books without one sit under "Other works." The two views can be combined.

Each title links to Jain Quantum's page viewer. A "text" tag links to the Quantum text where there is one (eLibrary texts have no public page); "PDF" and "docx" tags are inert, marking files that exist behind the library's login. "horizontal" marks a book from the "manuscripts" folder, and "API only" one that Jain Quantum does not list. A "TXT only" toggle hides everything without a text from either source.

Where the Text Stops

Jain Quantum's catalog runs to the end of , but its text stops earlier, and the library's own OCR stops in between. By when a book went online, there are three eras:

  • To : Quantum text. The newest Sanskrit book with a text on Quantum went online then.
  • To : eLibrary text only. books were OCR'd by the library, whose Word copy needs an account; Quantum lists those it has but shows no text for them. The Atlas fetched these copies, and the chart shows them in their own colour.
  • Since then: PDF only. The books added since have no OCR anywhere, public or not. of them, online from , are not in Quantum's catalog at all.

The library's OCR stops so cleanly because it was one batch, run on over everything then in the library (see PDFs and Two Text Sources), and has not been run since.

So the text series flattens while the library keeps growing, and the count of texts describes the library as it stood in mid-2022. The slow growth of the item series in recent years is partly an artefact of the cataloguing shift described above, which moves new Sanskrit editions out of this selection.

Further caveat: the bands describe each item as it stands today, not as it arrived. A book counted as having a text in the year it went online may have been OCR'd some time afterwards.
